November was established in 2025 as an independent research journal devoted to the study of light, geometry, and material performance in cold-climate architecture. Operating at the intersection of architectural history, building science, and urban morphology, the publication examines how built environments respond to extreme seasonal variation in solar availability.

Our editorial framework treats the city as an evolving thermodynamic laboratory. While contemporary architectural discourse often prioritizes high-transparency glass assemblies, our studies re-evaluate the enduring performance of heavy masonry, deep window reveals, and textured exterior surfaces. When low-angle winter sunlight strikes these traditional wall constructions, it creates complex micro-climatic zones of radiant warmth and pronounced visual contrast.

The journal publishes twice annually, coinciding with the autumnal equinox and the winter solstice. Contributions undergo rigorous peer review by architectural scholars, stone conservators, and solar geometry analysts. Field research is conducted across northern European, Baltic, and North American cities where winter solar angles fall below twenty-five degrees.

Through empirical measurement, high-resolution vector drawing, and archival investigation, November seeks to establish a renewed design vocabulary for cold-weather cities: one grounded in solar geometry, thermal inertia, and spatial permanence.
