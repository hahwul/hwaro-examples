Dalmoe is a dye room of two rooms and a walled yard, on the south slope above Seoksan-ri. The yard grows what the entries describe: a bed of jjok, a row of safflower, a gardenia hedge along the lane, and mugwort wherever it likes. The rooms hold nine onggi jars, two enamel pots, a well, and a long bench under the north window where the piecing is done.

## How the entries are kept

Every batch goes into a paper log: date, plant weight, cloth weight, water, temperatures, how long, and a scrap of the result stapled to the page. At the end of each season Han Seo-yun and Ok Min-jae read the log back and revise the entry for that plant. The date on an entry is the date of its last revision, not of its first writing.

The strip of patches at the head of each page stands for those stapled scraps: one dye, from its palest first dip to full strength.

## Conventions

- **Quantities** are given against the dry weight of the cloth. "300 g for 100 g" means three times the cloth's weight in plant.
- **Temperatures** are Celsius, read with a glass thermometer hung in the bath, not from the stove dial.
- **Water** is from the yard well: soft, pH 7.4, no iron to speak of. Hard or iron-bearing water will shift every colour here toward dull.
- **Korean terms** are romanised on first use with Hangul beside them, and used plainly afterwards.

## What is not here

Synthetic mordants, acid dyes, and anything the yard does not grow. Persimmon tannin is on the list for next autumn, once the two young trees fruit.
