Nothing in this room is dyed out of season. Mugwort is cut in May, before it buds. Safflower is picked at dawn through late June and into July, a basket a morning. The indigo comes down in the last week of July, and from then until the first cold night the jars are stirred daily and nobody travels. Leaf cut late or petals picked in the heat of the day give thin colour, and the batch log has the scraps stapled in to prove it.

Gardenia is the last thing in: the fruit is threaded on string in November and hung under the eaves, where it keeps for two years. After that the yard has nothing to give until April, and the bench turns to the cloth itself. Ramie is scoured and fed soy, scraps are sorted by weight and shade, and the seams get sewn. A wrapping cloth of forty patches takes most of a winter.
