Ssamsol is the flat-felled seam that joins every patch in a single-layer cloth. It encloses both raw edges, lies flat, and looks the same from either face, which matters when the cloth has no lining and will be seen against the light. Held up, each seam reads as a narrow darker band between two rows of stitches: four thicknesses of cloth where everywhere else there is one.

## Sewing it

1. Lay the two patches together with the lower one showing 3 mm beyond the upper. That gives 7 mm of allowance on the upper piece and 10 mm on the lower.
2. Sew a running stitch, *homjil* (홈질), along the 7 mm line at about ten stitches to 3 cm.
3. Fold the wider allowance over the narrower and crease it with a thumbnail. Dampen ramie first.
4. Open the patches flat and turn the wrapped allowance down to one side.
5. Fell the folded edge to the cloth with a small slanted hem stitch, *gamchimjil* (감침질), catching two threads of the ground each time.

The thread is usually a shade darker than the paler of the two patches.

## Order of piecing

Join scraps into strips, strips into blocks, blocks into the cloth. Stagger the joins. Where four seams meet at one point there are sixteen layers and the needle will not pass, so a good cloth has almost no crosses in it, only T-shaped meetings.

## What goes wrong

- **A ridge instead of a band.** The allowances were equal, so nothing wrapped. Offset them.
- **Seams that wave.** One patch was cut off-grain. Recut it on a pulled thread.
- **Puckers.** The felling stitches were pulled tight. They should lie on the cloth, not sink into it.
