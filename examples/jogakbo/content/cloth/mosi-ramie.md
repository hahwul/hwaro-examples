Mosi is woven from the bast of the ramie nettle. It is the stiffest, coolest and most open of the grounds we piece. A single layer held to a window shows every thread, and a seam shows as a clean darker line. That is the whole reason a jogakbo is made from it.

<!-- more -->

## The cloth

Ramie is cut three times a year, in June, August and October, and the August cut is the finest. The stalks are stripped, the bast split by hand into filaments, and the filaments spliced end to end into yarn without spinning.

Fineness is counted in *sae* (새): the number of 80-thread groups across a 30 cm loom width. Seven-sae cloth is everyday weight. Twelve-sae is fine enough to read through. We buy eight and nine, in bolts 31 cm wide.

## Before dyeing

New mosi carries bean-starch size from the loom and will not take dye evenly.

- Scour in ash lye, or 2 g of soda ash to the litre, at 60 °C for thirty minutes. Rinse until the water runs clear.
- For every dye except indigo, feed the cloth soy. Soak 100 g of soybeans overnight, grind them with 2 L of water, strain, and dip the cloth in the thinned milk. Dry it fully. Repeat once, then leave it a week.

Ramie is cellulose and gives plant dyes little to hold. The soy protein gives them something to bite.

## What goes wrong

- **Breaks along creases.** Dry ramie is brittle. Dampen it before folding, cutting, or finger-pressing a seam, and never iron a hard crease into dry cloth.
- **Pale colour that rubs off.** The soy was skipped, or the cloth was dyed the same day.
- **Fuzzing.** Too much wringing. Press the water out flat between towels.
