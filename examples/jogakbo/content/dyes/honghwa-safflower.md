Safflower petals hold two dyes. Most of it is a yellow that dissolves in plain water and is not worth keeping. Under it, perhaps one part in a hundred, is the red. All of the work is in losing the first without losing the second, and none of it involves heat.

<!-- more -->

## Petals

Pick in late June and July, at dawn, while the thistle heads are damp and the spines soft. Take only petals that have turned from yellow to orange-red. Dry them in shade. 300 g of dried petal dyes 100 g of silk a clear pink; a true red takes a kilo and three baths.

## Cold extraction

- Put the petals in a cloth bag and knead it under cold water, changing the water until it runs nearly clear. Allow two days and a dozen changes. A spoon of vinegar in the water keeps the red from leaving early.
- Squeeze the bag dry and knead it in 4 L of strained ash lye, pH 10 to 11, for half an hour. The liquor turns a muddy red-brown. Repeat once with fresh lye and combine the two.
- Add *omija* water or plum vinegar slowly, stirring, until the liquor clears to a bright blood red at about pH 6.
- Enter wetted silk at once. Work it for forty minutes at room temperature, never above 30 °C.
- Rinse in water soured with a little vinegar and dry in shade.

## What goes wrong

- **Salmon, not pink.** The yellow was not washed out. There is no fixing it afterwards.
- **Brown.** The bath was warm, or the lye sat on the petals for hours.
- **Colour gone after washing.** Soap is alkaline and takes the red straight back out. Wash honghwa cloth in plain cold water only.

On ramie the colour is pale and uneven. We keep honghwa for silk gauze.
