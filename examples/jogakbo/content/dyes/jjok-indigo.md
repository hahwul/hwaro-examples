Jjok is the only blue in the yard and the only dye here that never meets a mordant. The pigment has to be made insoluble, then made soluble again, and the cloth turns blue in the air, not in the jar.

<!-- more -->

## Harvest and paste

Sow in April and cut in late July, when the first flower spikes show but before they open. After flowering the leaf gives about a third less. Cut in the morning and have the stems under water within two hours.

- Pack 10 kg of fresh stems into an onggi jar, cover with 60 L of well water, and weight them down.
- Steep two days in a hot summer, three in a cool one, until the liquor is bottle green under a coppery film.
- Lift the stems out. Stir in 250 g of slaked shell lime and beat with a paddle for twenty minutes. The froth goes from green to blue to a dull purple.
- Let it settle overnight and pour off the brown water. The sludge is *niram* (니람), and it keeps for years.

## The vat

For a 40 L jar: 2 kg of niram, ash lye strained to pH 11, and a bowl of rice-wine lees to feed it. Hold it near 25 °C and stir once a day without breaking the surface. In ten to fifteen days the liquor turns yellow-green under a bronze skin and smells sweet rather than rotten.

Dip wetted cloth for five minutes, lift, and air it for ten. Six dips give a mid blue. Blue-black takes twenty or more, across several days.

## What goes wrong

- **Grey, chalky blue.** Too much lime in the paste. It does not wash out; make the next batch with less.
- **A vat that stays blue.** Too cold, or starved. Warm the jar and feed it. Do not add more lye.
- **Streaks.** The cloth went in dry or touched the sediment. Hang a basket a hand's width above the bottom.
