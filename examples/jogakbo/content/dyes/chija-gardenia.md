Chija is the easiest yellow we keep and the least permanent. The dried fruit of the gardenia hedge gives up its colour to warm water alone and takes on silk and ramie without a mordant. It also fades within a few summers if the cloth lives in a window.

<!-- more -->

## Fruit

Pick in late October and November, when the six-ridged pods have gone from green to a deep orange-red and the skin dents under a thumbnail. Thread them on string and dry them under the eaves for three weeks. They keep two years in a paper bag, though last year's fruit dyes a little browner than this year's.

## The bath

- Weigh fruit equal to the dry cloth. 100 g for 100 g gives a full egg-yolk yellow; 30 g gives a pale straw.
- Crack each pod and soak overnight in 3 L of cold water, then hold at 60 °C for forty minutes. Do not boil. Above 80 °C the liquor browns.
- Strain through a scrap of ramie. Enter wetted cloth at 40 °C, raise to 50 °C, and keep it moving for twenty-five minutes.
- Rinse once in cool water and dry in shade.

The same fruit will give a second, half-strength bath. An alum rinse afterwards, 3 g to the litre for ten minutes, pulls the yellow toward lemon but does little for fastness.

## What goes wrong

- **Blotches.** Gardenia strikes fast. Cloth that enters dry, or sits folded for a minute, keeps the mark.
- **Dull mustard.** Iron, from a rusty pot or the wrong water. Use the enamel pot.
- **Fading.** Expected. Here chija mostly goes under three dips of jjok to make green, where the indigo shades it from the light.
