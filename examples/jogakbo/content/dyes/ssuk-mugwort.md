Mugwort grows along the whole south wall and asks for nothing. It gives the quietest colours in the room: a greyed yellow-green with alum, a slate green with iron, and between them most of what the hillside behind the workshop looks like in March.

<!-- more -->

## Cutting

Take the top 30 cm of each plant in May and early June, before the flower buds form. Spring mugwort dyes green; the same plant cut in September dyes tan. Use it fresh, the same day, at three times the dry weight of the cloth: 300 g for 100 g.

## The bath

- Chop stem and leaf, cover with 5 L of water, and hold at 85 to 90 °C for fifty minutes. A rolling boil browns it.
- Strain. Enter wetted, mordanted cloth at 40 °C, raise to 70 °C over twenty minutes, and hold for twenty more.
- For a deeper shade, leave the cloth in the liquor overnight as it cools.

## Mordants

- **Alum**, 8 g for 100 g of cloth, before the bath. Soft sage, warm in tone.
- **Iron liquor**, made from rusty nails kept a month in rice vinegar. One tablespoon in 5 L, after the bath, for three minutes. The sage turns to slate green as you watch.

Ramie needs soy before any of this; see the mosi entry.

## What goes wrong

- **Khaki.** Late-season plants, or too hard a boil.
- **Charcoal, and cloth that tears.** Too much iron, or too long in it. Iron goes on working in the fibre for years, and heavily ironed ramie splits along its folds within a decade.
- **Patchy green.** The iron dip was not stirred. Three minutes, moving the whole time, then straight into clean water.
