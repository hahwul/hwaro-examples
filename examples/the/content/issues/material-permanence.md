Buildings and monumental objects are inevitably subject to atmospheric erosion. Rain, freeze-thaw expansion, and ultraviolet radiation test the physical integrity of exterior surfaces over centuries. Material selection determines whether weathering degrades a structure or enhances its character.

Metals such as copper, bronze, and weathering steel form protective oxide patinas upon atmospheric exposure. This self-passivating layer arrests deeper corrosion, sealing the core metal beneath a stable mineral skin. The greenish verdigris of copper roofs is not decay; it is an enduring shield against environmental moisture.

Limestone and granite weather through distinct chemical and physical pathways. Acidic rain gradually dissolves calcium carbonate in soft limestones, rounding sharp carved profiles over generations. Conversely, dense granites resist chemical erosion, retaining crisp tool marks and polished faces for millennia.

Designing for material permanence requires anticipating how water drains off facade planes. Drip edges, sloped window sills, and rainwater scuppers direct runoff away from vulnerable joints, allowing materials to age gracefully without staining underlying masonry walls.
