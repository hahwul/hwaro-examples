Light in architectural space has no independent physical body; it becomes visible only upon contact with solid mass. The orientation, depth, and splay of a window aperture determine how natural illumination penetrates interior chambers across solar seasons.

Deep masonry reveals act as natural light modulators. By angling the interior jambs of a narrow window opening, sunlight is refracted across textured plaster walls, turning harsh direct rays into a soft ambient glow that penetrates deep into structural interiors.

In classical Islamic architecture, intricate geometric screens carved from stone or stucco managed daylight with mathematical precision. These lattice structures scattered incoming solar rays into intricate shadow patterns, lowering interior thermal loads while maintaining privacy and ventilation.

Modern curtain wall glazing often eliminates shadow depth in favor of uninterrupted glass sheets. However, reintroducing deep reveals, solar louvers, and structural embrasures restores shadow as a fundamental spatial material, accentuating wall texture and marking the passage of time across interior surfaces.
