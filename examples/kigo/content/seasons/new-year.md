## What the first water carries

*Wakamizu* is the water drawn before anything else is done on the first morning of the year. In the old households it was the work of the eldest man, who went to the well in the dark, spoke to no one he met on the way, and carried the pail back for the first tea and the first offering. Very few of us have a well now. The word has outlived the well: it means the first water out of the kitchen tap as readily as it ever meant a bucket and a rope, and the saijiki has kept it under New Year, which it treats as a fifth season standing apart from winter.

What the word holds is not purity so much as order. On one morning a year the most ordinary act in the house is done on purpose, and done first. That is why the three poems here are so nearly empty. A pail, a sound, the cold going down. I have tried to leave the water alone and write only what it showed me: that the sky fits in a pail, that a morning can be one sound wide, and that the year begins somewhere under the ribs before it begins on the calendar.
