## Snow as an announcement

*Hatsuyuki* is the first snow of the winter, and the almanac does not care how much of it there is. A few minutes of flakes that melt on the road will count; so does the thin white line I found one December on the lid of the letterbox and nowhere else in the street. The word belongs to the family of firsts that the saijiki is so fond of, the first bonito, the first frost, the first cicada, and like all of them it records an event in the observer more than in the weather. Deep snow is a condition. First snow is a sentence someone says aloud.

That is why the middle poem is about climbing the stairs. In the house where I grew up, whoever saw it first was obliged to go and tell the others, and I have never been able to see first snow without wanting somebody to tell. The last poem is the other half of the word: it is nearly always gone by afternoon, and what it leaves is the ground looking darker and more exactly itself than it did the day before.
