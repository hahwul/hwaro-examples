## Rain that keeps an appointment

*Yūdachi* is written with the characters for evening and for standing up, and that is how it comes: the afternoon stands up all at once. The sky goes the colour of slate, the first drops fall far apart and as big as coins, and then for twenty minutes there is nothing to do but be wherever you happened to be. Then it is over, the gutters run, and the air is several degrees more forgiving than it was.

A summer season word has to carry heat without mentioning it, and this one does so by being the heat's only interruption. The poems are about what the shower arranges for us. It puts two strangers under one eave and gives them a reason to stand close without talking. It washes a stone road that nobody would have washed. It leaves a room in which a wind can finally be felt on the tatami. I think of the word as a courtesy the season pays: a fixed hour, almost daily in a good July, at which everyone is excused from what they were doing.
