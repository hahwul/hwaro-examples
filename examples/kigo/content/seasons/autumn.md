## The night gets its length back

The nights of autumn are not the longest of the year; those belong to the winter solstice. But the saijiki is a record of what is noticed, not of what is measured, and the length of the night is noticed in autumn, when the short nights of summer are still in the body and the lamp suddenly has to be lit before supper. So *yonaga*, the long night, is an autumn word, and its summer twin *mijikayo*, the short night, sits exactly opposite it in the book. *Nagaki yo*, in the first poem, is the same word turned around to fit the count.

What the word carries is surplus. There is an hour that was not there last month and nothing has been planned for it. People read, sew, listen. The poems in this part are indoor poems, which is unusual for me, and each has a small sound or a small lack at its centre: a page, rain heard in the dark, thread that will not reach the end of the seam. A long night does not have to be lonely. It only has to be longer than the task you brought to it, so that at some point you look up.
