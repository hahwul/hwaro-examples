## <span lang="ja">序</span> Preface

A saijiki is the haiku poet's almanac: a dictionary of the year in which every entry is a season word, and under every season word stand the poems that have used it. The great ones run to five volumes and several thousand words. This one has five words.

I made it over a single year, choosing one *kigo* for each of the traditional divisions (the New Year, which the almanac counts as a season of its own, and then spring, summer, autumn and winter) and writing only under that word until the season closed. Fifteen poems came through. They are set here the way they were written, in vertical columns read from the right, with a plain translation beside each and a short essay on what the word seems to me to carry.

Nothing in the book is rare. A pail of water, thin ice on a puddle, a shower, a lamp, snow on a letterbox. The almanac's whole argument is that these are enough, provided they arrive on time.

## <span lang="ja">凡例</span> Notes on reading

- *Columns.* Each poem stands in three. Begin at the top of the rightmost, read down, then move left.
- *Count.* The columns hold five, seven and five morae. A long vowel, a doubled consonant and the nasal *n* each take a full beat, which is why the romaji can look longer than it sounds.
- *Season word.* Every poem has exactly one. It is named at the head of its part, with its reading set small beside it.
- *Cutting word.* *Ya*, *kana* and *keri* do the work of a breath. The translations mark them with a colon, a dash, or silence.
- *Seal.* The red square under the frontispiece poem reads <span lang="ja">季</span>, season. It is used once.
