The two bowls for this jar were thrown in the last week of January, in a studio I could not get above eight degrees. Cold clay is slow and honest; it tells you every place your hands hesitated. The lower bowl went well. The upper one I threw the next morning with the stove lit behind me, and I did not notice until the join that the side nearest the heat had stiffened ahead of the other.

<!-- more -->

You can feel it when the halves meet. One side takes the slip and holds. The other side is still a little soft and gives under the weight, perhaps two millimetres, and you work the seam and tell yourself it has come level. It had not. The jar dried through February under cloth, and on the sixth of March, the day the ice let go of the stream and water ran under the studio door, I lit the kiln.

It came out with the left shoulder leaning very slightly outward, as if the jar had turned to listen to something. The join shows as a soft ridge you find with the flat of your hand before you find it with your eye.

I kept it because it was the first jar of the year and because the lean is so small that most visitors do not see it until I tell them, and then cannot stop seeing it. That seems to me the right size for a flaw.
