This is the small one, and I did not set out to make it. At the end of the 2023 batch of clay there was enough left in the bin for two bowls of about half the usual weight, and it seemed wrong to let it dry out. I threw them in an afternoon, joined them the next day without much thought, and put the jar at the back of the kiln floor where the large ones cast it into shadow.

<!-- more -->

The morning of the firing there was fog in the valley so thick that I found the woodpile by walking into it. Pine that has stood in fog burns cool and sulky for the first hours. The back of the kiln, where this jar sat, ran perhaps thirty degrees behind the front all the way to the top.

It is the most even jar I have made. The halves are within two millimetres of each other, the mouth is level, the join is where a join should be. For a week I thought it was the dullest thing on the shelf. Then I turned it round and found, low on the right side, a patch the size of my palm where the glaze had not fully melted. It is matt there, and faintly grey, like breath on a window.

I kept it for that patch. It is the place where you can see what the jar was before the fire finished with it, and on a jar this well behaved, it is the only place to look.
